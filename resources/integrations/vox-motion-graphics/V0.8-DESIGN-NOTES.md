# v0.8 design notes

## Evidence basis

v0.8 uses the mined Kodak production history as directional evidence, not as a template. The corpus contains 32 normalized decisions from one continuous documentary-motion project across several agent harnesses. Repeated user corrections and later retained implementations received the most weight. Early proposals, assistant-only suggestions, and parallel ideas without approval received less.

The strongest chain was consistent: technically valid scenes were still rejected as awkward; the work returned to an HTML storyboard; causal object relationships and clearer hierarchy were approved; later Remotion components retained those defining relationships. This supports workflow and editorial principles, not the source video's exact look.

## What changed the skill

- **Storyboard-first creative resolution:** HTML/CSS/JS preview became a first-class decision surface for composition, hierarchy, asset choice, scene structure, motion and transition intent, pacing, and readability. It is not expected to reproduce every Remotion control.
- **Editorial and technical QA are separate:** render, lint, timing, and asset validity are necessary but cannot approve hierarchy, clarity, continuity, or pacing.
- **Process visualization is causal:** systems, economics, dependencies, loops, and transformations should normally become flows, handoffs, accumulation/depletion, diagrams, or visible state changes instead of defaulting to cards or archival stills.
- **Anti-slideshow review is observable:** repeated cards, unchanged structure under changing narration, and slow zoom as the only motion are failure signals when the story needs progression.
- **Hierarchy precedes treatment:** every shot needs a focal point and dominant visual claim. Effects, labels, and secondary motion remain subordinate. Thesis/payoff frames usually simplify.
- **Voiceover defines a shared timing contract:** narration, scene duration, caption exits, transitions, and final hold are reviewed together.
- **Asset states are explicit:** candidate, generated/acquired, verified, and accepted are distinct. Technical or provenance verification does not equal editorial acceptance.
- **Failures remain teachable:** v0.8 preserves corrected failures alongside polished outcomes so agents can recognize symptoms and recovery paths.
- **HTML remains the creative authority:** structured handoff files are optional indexes/validation contracts, not a duplicate storyboard. This directly addresses drift and hallucination from maintaining the same creative decisions in multiple formats.
- **Automation needs bounded loops:** autonomous runs now have explicit repair limits, safe parallelism rules, resumable stage state, and a human approval handoff when requested.

## What was generalized

Project-specific scenes were converted into reusable relationships:

- a crowded historical opener became **claim and hierarchy before treatment**;
- a specification-heavy technical scene became **causal object chains**;
- a row of process cards became **continuous material or structural change**;
- a congested ending became **visual quiet at the thesis**;
- a plausible synthetic prop became **provenance-aware representation**;
- a long inactive ending became **voiceover/scene/caption/final-hold reconciliation**;
- repeated implementation churn became **storyboard critique, approval, then implementation freeze**.

These are preferred defaults with contextual exceptions. Cards can be correct for a deliberately card-based explainer; a still can hold when it remains editorially active; several simultaneous actions can work when hierarchy is clear.

## What was intentionally rejected

The following evidence was not promoted to universal behavior:

- exact people, dates, products, historical facts, prop order, and scene count;
- exact colors, textures, grain, scanlines, posterization, or named-creator surface styling;
- exact zoom ratios, frame numbers, durations, aspect ratio, frame rate, caption treatment, or audio levels;
- a universal ban on cards, still images, generated assets, or multiple simultaneous actions;
- Tailwind, Bun, a fixed preview port, or any other project-specific storyboard stack;
- the assumption that generated means approved, or that a successful render means editorial acceptance;
- unapproved parallel-agent proposals and earlier drafts superseded by later retained work.

## What still needs more data

- Whether continuous camera/object grammar remains preferable in comedic, abstract, musical, or typography-led work.
- Which observable properties users mean by “Vox-style” across multiple accepted references.
- When multi-action scenes remain clear because of hierarchy rather than low object count.
- How closely storyboard typography, easing, and audio must match final Remotion output to predict acceptance.
- Robust thresholds for dead tails, final holds, caption safe zones, asset halos/crops, audio loudness, and stillness detection.
- A reliable automated comparison between storyboard intent and implemented motion relationships.
- More accepted projects with different formats, pacing, visual languages, and audiences before promoting numeric presets.

## Evidence limitations

The sessions overlap and mostly describe one project, so event counts are not independent replications. Some “accepted” records mean a requested workflow was completed, not that every visual output was approved. Later retained implementation therefore outweighs an earlier completion report, and uncertainty remains explicit where approval could not be proven.
