# Motion Patterns

Use motion to clarify meaning, not to decorate a static composition.

## Joint shot score

Resolve these together before implementation; one short line per decision is enough:

| Decision | Answer |
|---|---|
| Composition | Dominant subject, viewpoint, and necessary context |
| Object action | Relationship that changes, and stable reference |
| Camera | New information revealed, or reason to remain locked |
| Attention | Look here before → during → after the action |
| Rhythm | What establishes, changes, resolves, and holds |
| Sound | Semantic accent, texture, bridge, or deliberate silence |
| Exit | Orientation carried forward, or contrast motivating a reset |

Object action and camera discovery are not interchangeable: finding a second object does not demonstrate replacement. Supporting motion and ambient life are optional; remove layers that compete with the assigned job.
Review shot scale, density, movement, and stillness against the film's progression. The sequence reference owns a full neighboring-scene scan. Repetition can establish a system or rhythm; contrast can mark a turn. Do not impose a variety quota, obligatory camera move, or fixed establish/action/hold formula.


## Semantic timing

After final VO alignment, bind each meaning-bearing event to an **orientation window → semantic trigger → action completion → readable payoff window → transition interval**. Before voice lock, these are provisional local estimates.

A noun may orient, a verb trigger action, a contrast introduce the counter-state, or a number lock into evidence. These are choices, not a requirement to animate each word. Record purposeful anticipation and J/L cuts when sound leads or trails the picture without confusing meaning. An offset alone is not a defect; an unexplained semantic mismatch is.

Measure the payoff from the first frame where essential content is actually readable, accounting for entrance motion and competing action—not from animation start. Inspect with speech at delivery size. No fixed hold duration proves readability. If the time envelope cannot support the shot, simplify it or deliberately reconform the edit; do not leave a nominal hold whose final state appears only at the cut.

When the final performance changes, reconform these events and reading windows, not just the scene endpoints.

When adding a beat to an accepted scene, give the new beat its own readable state. Do not stack another hero object over the existing focal subject.

## Entrance semantics

Choose deliberately:

- `synchronized`: one shared event, uniformity, or comparison;
- `staggered`: ordered accumulation, scale, or rhythm;
- `sequential`: the next state depends on the previous one.

Variation in direction, rotation, distance, depth, or settle is useful only when it supports the semantics. Repetition may be intentionally uniform.

## Camera grammar

Choose a camera behavior for the information it reveals, not as an obligatory effect. These names are starting points, not a whitelist or complete scene templates. A scene may contain several shots/beats; keep each camera intention coherent and resolve contradictory directions rather than appending both.

### Push-in / zoom-in

- **Use:** inspect a detail or intensify an established subject.
- **Behavior:** a push-in moves the camera toward the subject, changing depth relationships; a zoom-in increases magnification from a fixed viewpoint. Scaling a flat image is a crop zoom, not camera travel.
- **Needs:** geometry/depth for a spatial push; enough source detail and crop margin for a zoom.
- **Avoid/fallback:** do not promise new viewpoints from one flat image. Use an honest crop zoom or a locked detail when depth or resolution is insufficient.
- **Proof:** the intended detail becomes readable without clipping identity/evidence; inspect changing depth for a claimed push, not just a larger subject.

### Pull-back reveal

- **Use:** expose scale, surrounding context, or a consequence beyond an established detail.
- **Behavior:** withdraw the camera or open the crop/zoom; specify which. Keep the starting subject identifiable as previously hidden context enters view.
- **Needs:** supported surrounding content beyond the initial crop and a legible final composition.
- **Avoid/fallback:** shrinking a picture against empty space is not a context reveal. Do not invent evidence outside the source; use a supported wider view or a clearly illustrative diagram instead.
- **Proof:** playback establishes the detail, visibly reveals useful context, and leaves both the subject and the new relationship understandable.

### Orbit / object rotation

- **Use:** reveal meaningful sides, construction, or spatial relationships.
- **Behavior:** an orbit moves the camera around a subject; a turntable/object rotation keeps the camera fixed while the subject turns. Rotating a flat cutout in its plane is graphic rotation, not either spatial operation.
- **Needs:** suitable geometry or consistent supported views, coherent identity, and enough room for the intended view changes.
- **Avoid/fallback:** do not claim an unseen side from a single front-view PNG. Choose supported views, an honest graphic rotation, or limited parallax; none automatically proves full 3D form.
- **Proof:** check the intended viewpoint/side changes, plausible occlusion, stable identity and readable settled labels; distinguish camera travel from subject movement.

### Parallax

- **Use:** establish depth or separate a focal subject from its environment.
- **Behavior:** depth-separated layers move at different rates or scales under a coherent camera-like move; they are not one uniformly transformed bitmap.
- **Needs:** separable depth layers, appropriate occlusion order, and crop/edge coverage throughout the move.
- **Avoid/fallback:** no fabricated hidden evidence or unsupported full-viewpoint promise. If layers cannot support the move, reduce travel or use a crop zoom/locked composition.
- **Proof:** inspect relative layer motion and every revealed edge for gaps, clipping, contradictory depth, or distraction from the assigned visual job.

### Dolly zoom

- **Use:** convey tension or a change in perceived spatial scale when perspective distortion serves the story.
- **Behavior:** couple camera travel with compensating focal-length change so the focal subject stays approximately the same screen size while background perspective changes.
- **Needs:** depth-aware geometry and coupled camera/lens control, or an explicitly illustrative 2.5D approximation with independently controlled layers.
- **Avoid/fallback:** uniform scaling is not a dolly zoom; a flat image cannot supply real perspective change. Prefer an ordinary push/pull or describe the approximation honestly; do not warp evidence to manufacture drama.
- **Proof:** subject size remains approximately stable while the background relationship visibly changes, without identity distortion or unreadable critical content.

### Locked shot / evidence hold

- **Use:** let an action explain itself, make evidence readable, build anticipation, or give emotion/payoff room.
- **Behavior:** a locked shot fixes the camera while objects may act; an evidence hold keeps the essential source content stable enough to inspect.
- **Needs:** a useful composition and adequate reading/observation time at delivery size.
- **Avoid/fallback:** do not add drift just to satisfy a motion quota or hold unrelated imagery under a new idea. Recompose or cut only when the assigned job requires it.
- **Proof:** review with narration/captions to confirm the action or evidence reads and the hold remains purposeful; stillness is not a missing technique.

Lateral travel through a process/timeline/geography and detail-to-system dives remain useful choices. Resolve their path, stable reference, information revealed, and prerequisites with the same procedure. Prefer one signature mechanism plus subordinate motion; remove camera drift that discovers nothing.

## Reusable mechanics

These are primitives, not complete shots:

- object/frame becomes the next world;
- path draw or route trace;
- accumulation/depletion system;
- network/flywheel loop;
- bottleneck/funnel;
- object-as-chart;
- physical replacement or handoff;
- evidence crop/inspection;
- differential 2.5D depth with contact shadow;
- light/focus reveal;
- damped gesture;
- deterministic flicker or registration jitter;
- count-up tied to verified data.

Prefer one signature mechanism plus supporting motion.

## Ambient life and loops

Ambient motion may include subtle drift, boil, light, focus, route pulses, smoke, or deterministic grain. It must remain subordinate.

For continuous loops, introduce or recycle the replacement before the current layer exits. Inspect frames around the seam. Never assume an off-screen reset is invisible.

## Stillness decision

Stillness is intentional when it:

- lets evidence become readable;
- builds anticipation;
- gives an emotional or thesis payoff room;
- creates contrast before the next action.

Record the purpose. If narration changes idea while composition, focal point, and object relationships remain unchanged, check whether the held image still performs its assigned job. Sustained testimony, identity, emotion, or evidence inspection can remain valid across multiple spoken facts.


## Data and Time Compression

A count, clock, progress readout, or timeline can represent a real duration or quantity without playing in real time. Make the editorial compression clear.

- Do not imply one-to-one elapsed time unless the scene actually uses it.
- If many units pass faster than the eye can parse, prefer grouped increments, a rolling transition, a jump with a clear landing, or a labeled compression.
- Do not fire one SFX per unit at rates that collapse into noise. Accent meaningful thresholds or the final value.
- The final number must remain readable long enough to function as evidence.
