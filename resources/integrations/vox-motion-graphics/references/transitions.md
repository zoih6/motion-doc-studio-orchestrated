# Attention Continuity and Transitions

Use this reference to connect scenes and states without losing the viewer's gaze.

## Plan the handoff

For important boundaries, decide whether attention continues, matches, contrasts, resets, or hands off. A transition may preserve orientation, reveal connection, change scale/era, create contrast, or give a payoff room; a deliberate hard cut, archival/sound-led bridge, or held pause may be the clearest choice.

At scene level identify:

- entry and exit focal subject/zone;
- dominant direction and scale;
- object, line, shape, color, or idea that may continue.

The next state may begin near the previous focal exit, continue or reverse motion, carry or transform an object/line/shape, contrast macro and system scale, or hard-cut when a conceptual break should feel decisive. Continuity is a heuristic; a deliberate reset is valid.

## Transition intent

A transition should do at least one useful job:

- preserve orientation;
- reveal a causal connection;
- change scale or viewpoint;
- mark a new state or era;
- create contrast;
- give a payoff room to land.

If it only advertises an effect, remove or simplify it.

## Useful structures

- object becomes portal/environment;
- path or line continues into a new system;
- outgoing object exits where the next enters;
- camera pulls from detail to system, or dives from system to detail;
- shape or crop match preserves a semantic object;
- sound attack bridges the cut;
- deliberate still hold followed by a hard state change.

## Named transition vocabulary

Choose by the boundary's editorial job, then resolve the visible behavior and prerequisites. These are options, not required effects or a fixed transition catalog. Use the [camera vocabulary](motion-patterns.md#camera-grammar) to distinguish viewpoint changes from image/panel movement.

### Whip-pan / directional slide

- **Use:** carry energy or attention in a chosen direction between related states.
- **Behavior:** a whip-pan uses a rapid camera-style sweep, often with motion blur, landing on the incoming view; a directional slide translates a panel/object/image without claiming camera travel through space.
- **Needs:** outgoing/incoming views, sufficient edge coverage, a planned direction/focal landing, and readable states before and after the sweep.
- **Avoid/fallback:** a sliding card is not automatically a whip-pan. Do not combine a locked-camera instruction with a pan on the same beat; choose one, separate their windows, or use a direct cut. Never blur essential evidence during its only readable window.
- **Proof:** inspect the full boundary clip for a coherent sweep/slide, gaps or flashes, focal landing, and a readable payoff—not just matching endpoint frames.

### Match cut

- **Use:** connect or contrast ideas through a recognizable shape, object, composition, or action.
- **Behavior:** cut between distinct shots at a chosen visual match; a match cut does not require a continuous morph.
- **Needs:** a clear matching anchor and framing/action phase that makes the intended connection perceptible.
- **Avoid/fallback:** a visual match is not factual proof of causation or shared identity. If it confuses the argument, use a deliberate reset rather than forcing continuity.
- **Proof:** watch across the cut for recognition of the intended match and an honest relationship; preserve enough context to distinguish the two shots.

### J-cut / L-cut

- **Use:** let sound anticipate the next view or carry the outgoing thought into the next picture.
- **Behavior:** a J-cut brings incoming audio before its picture; an L-cut retains outgoing audio after the picture changes.
- **Needs:** meaningful audio overlap, intact speech/caption timing, and a visual handoff that preserves speaker/source and argument clarity.
- **Avoid/fallback:** do not truncate speech, overlap unintelligible dialogue, or imply an unsupported speaker/source association. Use a straight cut when the bridge confuses meaning.
- **Proof:** audition the mastered boundary with captions and picture; verify the intended lead/tail, intelligibility, and readable essential content.

### Hard cut / hold-to-cut

- **Use:** mark contrast, a new state/era, or a decisive payoff without an effect.
- **Behavior:** switch directly between views; hold-to-cut first lets the outgoing evidence/action/payoff settle, then makes the break.
- **Needs:** an understandable outgoing contribution and an incoming composition that promptly orients attention.
- **Avoid/fallback:** no arbitrary hold or mandatory smoothing effect. Fix an unreadable outgoing payoff or confusing incoming view before adding a transition.
- **Proof:** review with sound at consumption size for enough outgoing reading time and a clear intentional reset; a direct cut is not failed animation.


## Freeze and implementation

The storyboard freeze captures transition type, focal handoff, defining relationship, rough pacing, and sound intent. Record only the defining **observable invariant + proof location**: for example, the same object stays registered while its carrier departs, or the broad starting state is visible before narrowing. Do not inventory every pixel.

Inspect short boundary clips spanning before, during, and after the handoff against the frozen revision. Endpoint frames cannot prove spatial continuity, timing, or an audible cue. Include the mastered sound when it carries the transition; record each planned cue as implemented, deliberately replaced, or explicitly omitted with a reason.

Remotion may refine easing and overlap, but changing the editorial job returns to review. A reviewed hard-cut replacement is valid; silent drift is not. Contrast transitions need not preserve exact transforms. Intentional J/L cuts and anticipation may cross scene boundaries when orientation and meaning survive.
